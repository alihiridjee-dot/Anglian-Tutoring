## 8. AI marking gives marks for wrong science (found 27 Oct, nothing fixed yet)

**What happened.** Finley Culverhouse handed in "AQA 1.1 Eukaryotes and prokaryotes" on 10 Oct (submission `6679e403-7820-4af5-8477-5742cf771367`). On Q2 ("three differences between a bacterial cell and an animal cell", 3 marks) he wrote "eukaryotic cells have no cell wall where bacteria cells do". That is false, because plant and fungal cells are eukaryotic and have walls. The AI gave 3/3. Its comment rewrote the point as "a cell wall only in the bacterial cell", which is true, so Finley was praised for a sentence he never wrote and still holds the wrong idea. The same task's Q4 and Q5 exist to test exactly this misconception. No tutor saw it: `graded_by` and `tutor_reviewed_at` are empty, and it was released 30 minutes after marking.

**Why it got through.**

1. The marking prompt (`SYSTEM` in `supabase/functions/mark-homework/index.ts`, around line 105) says "Credit correct science expressed in the student's own words" but never says that a statement that is false as written earns no mark. So the model reads answers charitably and fills in what the student "meant" ("eukaryotic" read as "animal").
2. The generated mark scheme says "Allow the converse for the animal cell if the comparison is clear", which the model took as permission.
3. The prompt doesn't make the comment quote the student, so the comment can quietly correct the error instead of naming it.
4. AI marks go to the student after `REVIEW_WINDOW_MINUTES = 30` whether or not a tutor looked.

**Also seen on the same hand-in (lower priority).**

- Q2: his first and third points are both the nucleus idea ("eukaryotes have a nucleus", then "bacterial DNA is a single loop, the animal's is in an enclosed nucleus"). The scheme has two overlapping bullets (no nucleus / single loop vs chromosomes in a nucleus), so one idea can earn two marks. Check whether the practice queue's scheme writer produces overlapping bullets elsewhere.
- Q3(a): a mark was taken for writing 2.0 × 10⁻⁶ without "m", although the question itself says "in metres". That's arguable. Decide whether the scheme's "no unit, no mark" rule should apply when the question fixes the unit.

**Fix, in this order.**

1. **Prompt rules** in `mark-homework` (code change + edge function deploy, see the edge-functions recipe in memory):
   - A statement that is scientifically wrong as written earns no mark, even if a nearby correct idea is on the scheme. Don't guess what the student meant when the words say something false.
   - A wrong general claim (e.g. "eukaryotic cells have no cell wall") is never accepted as the "converse" of a specific one ("animal cells have no cell wall").
   - One idea earns one mark, even if it fits two scheme bullets.
   - Each comment names any false statement the student made and why it's wrong, and describes the student's point in their own words, never a corrected version.
   Test it without paid runs where possible. Any real model call to check the change needs Ali's yes (AGENTS.md rule 12).
2. **Correct Finley's record**, after Ali's yes (it's a real student's mark): Q2 becomes 2/3 with a comment that plant cells have walls; recompute `score_pct` (15/17 → 14/17, 88% → 82%) and fix the summary if it mentions Q2. Consider telling Finley or his parent that the mark changed.
3. **Audit other AI-marked answers** for the same pattern (full marks on a long answer containing a false general claim). Read-only first; show Ali the list before changing any marks.
4. **Ali's call:** should AI marks still auto-release after 30 minutes with no tutor check, at least until the fix above has been proven?
